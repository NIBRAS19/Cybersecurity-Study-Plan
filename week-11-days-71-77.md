* What it is: your capstone needs a single target application with both traditional web attack surface (login, data access, forms) AND an AI/LLM component (chatbot, RAG-based search, AI-powered feature) to genuinely exercise the full curriculum

### Concept 2 — Writing the Complete Scope Document
* Definition: a formal document defining exactly what's in scope (the target application, its components), what testing domains will be covered (web OWASP Top 10, LLM OWASP Top 10, threat modeling, CI/CD review), and what's explicitly out of scope
* Practical application: mirror Day 26's Month 1 capstone checklist structure, but now include a second major section for AI-specific testing (drawing on your complete Week 9–10 toolkit)
* Best practices: an explicit, written scope is standard professional practice for any real security engagement — this document itself is portfolio evidence of professional rigor

### Concept 3 — Time-Boxing Across a Broader Scope
* Key terminology: multi-domain time allocation
* Practical application: with 5 days (72–76) for actual testing/building work, plan roughly: Day 72 for web OWASP Top 10 testing, Day 73 for AI/LLM OWASP Top 10 testing, Day 74 for threat modeling + CI/CD review, Days 75–76 for report writing — directly recalling Day 26's time-boxing discipline, now applied across a wider domain

# 🧠 3. Specific Subtopics & Concepts

### Concept 1 — Final Polish
* What it is: a careful, complete proofreading pass of the entire capstone report, checking for consistency, clarity, and any remaining rough edges
* Why it matters: as your flagship, most complex portfolio piece, this document deserves the most careful final polish of the three
* Best practices: read it aloud if possible — this often catches awkward phrasing or unclear logic that silent reading misses

### Concept 2 — Top-Level Portfolio Integration
* Definition: creating (or updating) a top-level README/landing page for your overall GitHub portfolio that presents all three pieces together as one coherent career-transition narrative
* Practical application: structure as — a brief personal/professional summary → Portfolio Piece #1 (AppSec) → Portfolio Piece #2 (DevSecOps) → Portfolio Piece #3 (AI Security) → each with a one-line description and a link
* Best practices: this top-level view is often the very first thing a hiring manager sees when they click through to your GitHub from your resume/LinkedIn — it deserves as much care as any individual piece

### Concept 3 — The Complete Interview Narrative
* Key terminology: flexible-depth narrative
* Practical application: prepare a narrative that can flex — a 30-second version ("I built three portfolio projects covering application security, DevSecOps, and AI security, culminating in a comprehensive assessment of an AI-integrated web application"), a 2-minute version (one sentence per major project), and the full 5-minute deep-dive on the capstone specifically if an interviewer wants to go deeper
* Best practices: practice all three lengths — interviewers have wildly varying amounts of time and interest, and a flexible narrative serves every scenario well

### Key Terms to Remember

| Term | Meaning | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| Final polish pass | Careful complete proofreading of the flagship document | Your most complex piece deserves the most careful final review |
| Top-level portfolio integration | A unified README presenting all three pieces as one narrative | Often the first thing a hiring manager sees — deserves equal care |
| Flexible-depth narrative | A story that works at 30 seconds, 2 minutes, or 5 minutes | Serves the wide variance in real interview time/interest |
